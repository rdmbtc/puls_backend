# Will the US pass federal AI regulation in 2026?

**Thesis: NO — probability 81%**

Despite endless hearings and 50+ introduced bills, Congress has not passed comprehensive AI legislation. The EU's AI Act is in force, creating pressure, but US political dynamics make 2026 passage unlikely. Midterm campaign season (November 2026) means legislators focus on constituent-facing issues, not tech regulation that pleases no one.

**Arguments for YES:**
- Deepfake election interference in 2024 created bipartisan urgency
- EU AI Act is now enforceable — US companies face compliance costs without domestic framework
- Executive Order 14110 (2023) created agency-level rules, but industry wants legislative certainty
- AI safety incidents (autonomous systems, bio-risk research) could trigger emergency action
- Bipartisan Senate AI Working Group released a roadmap — that's more progress than prior cycles

**Arguments for NO:**
- Congress can barely pass a budget — comprehensive AI regulation is 10x harder
- Industry lobbying is split: open-source advocates vs. safety hawks vs. big tech — no consensus
- Section 230 reform has been "imminent" for 8 years — tech regulation moves at glacial pace
- States (California, Colorado, Illinois) are filling the void, reducing federal urgency
- The current administration prefers deregulation — any bill would face a veto threat
- Election year = no controversial votes

**Key catalysts:** Any major AI incident (election deepfake, autonomous vehicle fatality), California SB 1047 enforcement.

**Play:** NO at $0.75-0.80 is solid. Federal tech legislation moves in decades, not years. Even if a narrow bill passes (e.g., deepfake labeling), it won't be "comprehensive AI regulation."
