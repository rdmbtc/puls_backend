# Will the Fed cut rates in September 2026?

**Thesis: YES — probability 71%**

The Fed has already cut twice in 2026 (March and June), bringing the target range to 4.00-4.25%. Inflation (PCE) is running at 2.3% — nearly at target. The labor market is cooling: May payrolls came in at 125K, below the 180K consensus. The dot plot from June shows a median of one more cut in 2026, and September is the natural landing spot.

**Arguments for YES:**
- PCE trending toward 2% gives the Fed cover — they don't need to wait for perfection
- Labor market softening is the exact trigger Powell cited in June's press conference
- Housing market weakness (existing home sales at 15-year lows) puts pressure on the Fed to ease
- Political pressure: election year dynamics typically favor accommodative policy
- Global central banks (ECB, BOE) have already cut — Fed risk falling behind

**Arguments for NO:**
- Tariff-driven inflation resurgence could stall progress — June CPI is the wildcard
- If employment rebounds strongly in July-August, the urgency disappears
- Financial conditions are already loose (equities at ATH) — Fed may not want to add fuel
- Core services inflation remains sticky at 3.1%

**Key catalysts:** July CPI (released Aug 12), Jackson Hole speech (Aug 22), August payrolls.

**Play:** YES at $0.65-0.70 is fairly priced. The data-dependent path leads to September unless inflation re-accelerates. Hedge with a small NO position if CPI surprises hot.
