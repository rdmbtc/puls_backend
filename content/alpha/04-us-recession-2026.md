# Will the US enter a recession in 2026?

**Thesis: NO — probability 74%**

The US economy has defied recession calls for three years straight. Q1 2026 GDP grew at 1.8% — soft but positive. Consumer spending remains resilient (May retail sales +0.3% MoM), and the labor market, while cooling, hasn't cracked. The yield curve uninverted in late 2025, and historically recession follows 6-18 months after — we're in that window, but leading indicators aren't flashing red.

**Arguments for YES:**
- Leading Economic Index (LEI) has declined for 14 consecutive months — longest streak outside a recession
- Commercial real estate distress is spreading: office vacancy at 20%, regional bank exposure unresolved
- Consumer credit card delinquencies hit 3.2% — highest since 2011
- If the Fed overtightens or keeps rates too high for too long, something breaks
- China/EU slowdown drags US exports

**Arguments for NO:**
- Services sector (78% of GDP) continues expanding — ISM Services at 53.8
- AI investment boom: capex from hyperscalers adds ~0.5% to GDP growth directly
- Fiscal stimulus hasn't fully unwound — CHIPS Act and IRA spending peaks in 2026-27
- Housing starts recovering as mortgage rates ease toward 6%
- Labor market is rebalancing through reduced hiring, not layoffs

**Key catalysts:** July GDP advance estimate, any credit event (regional bank, CRE default).

**Play:** NO at $0.70-0.75 is reasonable. The soft landing narrative has momentum, but tail risk from CRE or credit markets keeps it honest.
